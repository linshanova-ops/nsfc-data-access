Urban environmental stress and mental illness

Identification and molecular characterisation of urban-environmental stress patterns affecting mental illness

识别城市环境压力模式对精神障碍的影响及其分子机制

The scientific aim is a fine-grained, real-time characterisation of stressful events in urban living conditions, taking in both objective factors and individual perceptions, and how each progresses through everyday life. That characterisation identifies the environmental triggers of stress, their genetic and molecular mediators, and their effects on behaviour, physiological measures, and mental illness. It is meant to map the urban conditions that induce stress, to understand the biological mechanisms, and to follow the consequences for mental illness, so the findings can be turned into prevention and intervention, from urban planning, public health, and policy to the care of individual patients.

Three objectives follow. The first measures real-time urban environmental stress with the StreetMind application and wearable sensors, in healthy adults and in people with stress-related mental disorders. The second relates those exposures to mental health in major depressive disorder, anxiety disorder, alcohol use disorder, and healthy controls, by linking daily reports to geospatial environmental data, and tests the findings in external cohorts. The third identifies biological mechanisms, through genetics, DNA methylation, gene expression, and proteomics, and joins those molecular profiles to the environmental stress patterns and to how the illness unfolds.

The host is Fudan University, through Huashan Hospital. The Centre for Population Neuroscience and Stratified Medicine (PONS) and the Institute of Science and Technology of Brain-inspired Intelligence (ISTBI) sit within Huashan Hospital. The clinical partners are Shanghai Mental Health Center and Peking University Sixth Hospital.

Motives

Why this study

Environmental factors contribute more than 40% to depression, anxiety, and alcohol use disorder, and they are closely tied to relapse and to how the illness turns out. Urban life is one driver, but the everyday conditions that make up that life have not been characterised at the scale and speed at which they occur. A record that captures the complexity and the movement of urban living is still missing. The biological mediators of those conditions, and how far they change the course of illness, remain unclear. Leaving them out of account makes relapse harder to interrupt and recovery harder to sustain.

Much of the existing evidence comes from a general self-report, or from a questionnaire completed away from the exposure. Those methods miss the moment. They also miss the difference between what the environment is and what the person feels. Noise, air, crowding, a commute, and a place someone returns to can each change stress, and they do not do so in the same way, or at the same time, for every person. Digital tools used in daily life can hold the psychological report and the physiological response together, at the time they happen.

The study joins the parts shown in the study-design figure. StreetMind, delivered in this project as iDianji, records three modules across four weeks. My Spots logs places a person goes and how those places feel. My Route logs a usual journey, the conditions along it, and how connected the person feels. My Day logs the day as a whole: mood, sleep, activity, nutrition, company, time in green space and by water, noise, temperature and air, and substance use. Prompts are started by the participant, started when heart rate departs from that person’s baseline, or delivered as random checks. A Huawei Watch GT6, worn for the same four weeks, records heart rate, blood oxygen, a stress index, sleep, movement, and location. environMAP then links those locations to urbanicity and land cover, natural space, climate and weather, air pollution, and regional socioeconomic conditions. Blood and urine, taken once at the baseline visit, support genetics, DNA methylation, gene expression, and proteomics. The end visit repeats the clinical assessment. Held together, these components are meant to show how a distinct urban stress pattern is mediated biologically and how it relates to mental illness.

The dataset

The NSFC dataset

The NSFC dataset is the data generated under grant W2541022. It joins four collection systems: the electronic data capture system (EDC), which holds registration, demographic and medical history, clinical follow-up, medication, adverse event, biosample and hair-sample records, and the login-code mapping; the iDianji application, which holds ecological momentary assessment and smartphone sensing; Huawei wearable devices carried through Huawei Health, which hold continuous physiology, sleep, physical activity and location; and the biosample and omics pipelines. Each system writes its own tables with its own participant key. The coordinating centre holds the mapping between keys and issues research extracts in which direct identifiers are replaced by an anonymous participant code.

Study design

The design below is the approved research plan. It is the target for the study as a whole.

The project will recruit 680 adults aged 18–50 years, with a balanced sex distribution, from outpatient services in Shanghai and Beijing. Instruments

Tools

The study uses a small set of instruments, each aimed at a different part of the same four weeks. They are the tools named in the research plan.

StreetMind, delivered as iDianji

StreetMind was designed in the Horizon Europe project environMENTAL, under the leadership of Professor Gunter Schumann, to record frequently visited places, routes, and daily experience, with geo-tracked emotions, actions, and behaviour. This project delivers it as iDianji and extends it with wearable physiology and with environMAP. The three modules are My Spots, My Route, and My Day. Participants rate emotional state, say whether a change in heart rate came from emotion, activity, or an unknown cause, and answer questions on recent activity and social context. A free-text field records the account in the person’s own words. Prompts are self-initiated, triggered when heart rate departs from that person’s baseline, or delivered as random checks. The phone also records location, activity state, accelerometer readings, and foreground application use.

Huawei Watch GT6

Each participant wears a Huawei Watch GT6 for the four weeks, carried through Huawei Health. It records heart rate by photoplethysmography, blood oxygen saturation, a device stress index, nightly sleep with stages, steps, distance, energy expenditure, physical activity sessions, and device location. The watch supplies the continuous physiological record that the momentary questions sit against.

environMAP

environMAP is the geospatial data hub developed in environMENTAL, with the Department of Earth System Science at The University of Hong Kong. Its spatial data infrastructure was built at the Berlin Institute of Health at Charité. It holds curated layers for urbanicity and land cover, natural space, climate and weather, air pollution, and regional socioeconomic status, with a catalogue for choosing layers and a service for displaying them. Authorised staff link a participant’s locations and StreetMind responses to those layers. The hub is not a file the participant carries.

Clinical and psychological instruments

The Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview confirms major depressive disorder, anxiety disorder, or alcohol use disorder. Repeated scales are the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test, the Patient Health Questionnaire-9, and the Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale, each twice a week, with cue-induced craving on a visual analogue scale. The Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress is given at the beginning and at the end. One or two Perceived Stress Scale items are given daily, and the full scale twice a week. Surveys cover urbanicity, eco-anxiety, and mental resilience. Participants are also asked for feedback on the monitoring.

Electronic data capture

Clinical visits are recorded in an electronic case report form. The form holds registration, demographic and medical history, clinical follow-up, medication, adverse events, and the biosample and hair-sample records. Every record carries the participant’s anonymous code.

Biosample assays

Blood and urine are taken once, at the baseline visit. Twenty-four millilitres of blood are split across EDTA tubes for genotyping and storage, heparin tubes for plasma protein, and Tempus tubes for RNA. The planned assays are the Illumina Chinese Genotyping Array, Illumina EPIC DNA methylation at about 935,000 sites, Illumina NovaSeq RNA sequencing, and plasma proteomics by mass spectrometry or antibody-based assays.

How the study runs

Procedures

The study is a four-week, multi-centre protocol in outpatient services in Shanghai and Beijing. The centres are Huashan Hospital of Fudan University, Shanghai Mental Health Center, and Peking University Sixth Hospital. A coordination group of clinical trial coordinators from the sites oversees recruitment, clinical evaluation, and follow-up. Trained psychiatrists explain the study, including its possible benefits and risks. People who are interested are screened, and those who are eligible give written informed consent before enrolment. Each person receives one anonymous code, used on every record.

Who can take part

Patients meet ICD-10 or DSM-5 criteria for major depressive disorder, an anxiety disorder, or alcohol use disorder. Comorbidity is allowed. Age is 18–50 years, with a balanced sex distribution. Participants need ordinary cognitive ability and the ability to use an Android smartphone. They agree to wear the study watch, download the research application, and upload data. Anyone taking medication must have been on a stable dose of a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, in particular citalopram or escitalopram, for at least one week, with a steady-state concentration reached before enrolment. People with alcohol use disorder must have completed alcohol withdrawal, as judged by clinical criteria or by the treating clinician. Healthy controls are held to the same age, cognitive, device, medication, and consent rules, and to the same exclusions.

Who is not enrolled

The plan excludes current use of benzodiazepines or opiates; current brain-stimulation treatment, including transcranial magnetic stimulation or electroconvulsive therapy; a skin infection or a significant skin defect on the wrist or arm that would stop the watch sitting properly; use in the past 30 days of a medicine that strongly inhibits or induces CYP450, or a dose that has not yet reached steady state; and a serious physical illness, such as a brain tumour or brain injury, or a current pregnancy or breastfeeding, where that would interfere with the protocol.

What each person does

Screening uses the Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview to confirm the diagnostic group. After consent, the baseline visit records demographics, medical, surgical, family, and social history, allergies, and current and past medication with dose and duration. The Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress and the repeated psychological scales are given at this visit. Blood and urine are taken once, at the start. The participant then downloads iDianji and wears the Huawei Watch GT6 for four weeks. During those weeks the application asks about places, routes, and the day, on a schedule of self-initiated reports, reports triggered when heart rate leaves that person’s baseline, and random control prompts. At the end of the four weeks the clinical assessment is repeated, including the Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress.

Blood and urine

Each person gives 24 mL of blood, split into six tubes of 4 mL: two EDTA tubes for genotyping and for storage, two heparin tubes for plasma protein, and two Tempus tubes for RNA. Heparin blood is centrifuged within 30 minutes of collection, at 3,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C, and the plasma is taken from the supernatant. EDTA and Tempus tubes are stored at −80°C within two hours of collection. Urine is collected at the same visit. Samples are kept for genotyping, RNA sequencing, epigenome-wide methylation, and proteomics.

Governance

Legal and regulatory requirements

Release of these data is governed by the law of the People’s Republic of China. The host institution holds the data and carries the statutory duties. Approval by the access-review group is a scientific approval; it does not by itself satisfy a statutory requirement, and no extract is released until the host institution has cleared the requirements that apply to that request.

Ethics review of the secondary use

Reusing participant data is research involving human participants. The institution that provides the data must have the proposed use reviewed by its ethics committee and must fix by agreement the scope of use, how the data are handled, and how they are disposed of at the end (Measures for the Ethics Review of Life Science and Medical Research Involving Humans, 2023, Article 28). Where a study qualifies for expedited review or for exemption, the committee records that decision; exemption does not apply where sensitive personal information is involved. Journals ask authors to show that the study was approved (Article 29), so the manuscript must cite the approval that covers this use. For international scientific cooperation, each party must obtain ethics review in its own country or region (Implementing Rules, National Health Commission Order No. 16, Article 30). The institution itself must designate the department responsible for human genetic resources, keep an activity ledger, and review the purpose and protocol of each study (Article 10).

Human genetic resource information

Under the Implementing Rules of the Regulation on the Administration of Human Genetic Resources (National Health Commission Order No. 16, published 5 November 2026 and in force from publication, Article 3), human genetic resource information comprises human gene, genome, transcriptome and epigenome nucleic-acid sequence information and the disease information associated with it; pure clinical, imaging, protein and metabolic data are excluded. In this dataset, genotypes, DNA methylation and RNA sequencing are therefore human genetic resource information. Plasma proteomics is not, but it remains sensitive personal information. A collection license is required for large population studies of more than 10,000 participants and for collection within the published lists of important genetic families or specific regions (Article 16); planned enrolment of 680 is below that threshold. Splitting a collection, or reporting it in parts, to avoid a license is expressly prohibited (Article 16).

Provision to an overseas recipient

Only a Chinese unit may provide Chinese human genetic resources overseas; overseas individuals and foreign units may not (Article 9). A foreign unit includes an entity established, actually controlled, or indirectly held to the extent of 50% or more by an overseas organization or individual (Article 59). Providing or opening human genetic resource information to such a recipient requires prior reporting to the National Health Commission with an information backup, stating the purpose and use, the backup, the recipient, and a risk assessment (Article 39). Two routes are simplified. Information provided for publication or academic conference exchange completes the prior report when the data are deposited with the China National Center for Bioinformation before the paper is published or the conference takes place (Article 40). Within an approved international cooperation, information may be provided to a foreign recipient named in the application and covered by the cooperation agreement without a separate report or backup (Article 41). A change of purpose or recipient after a report has been made requires a new prior report before the change takes effect (Article 42). The revised Rules no longer state a numerical security-review threshold; under Article 28 of the Regulation, a provision that may affect public health, national security or the public interest must pass a security review. Until the host institution has confirmation from the National Health Commission, any request that would give an overseas recipient genomic data is treated as requiring that review.

Personal information and cross-border access

A psychiatric diagnosis and a location trajectory are sensitive personal information under the Personal Information Protection Law. Letting an overseas body access or process data held on a server in China counts as providing data overseas, even though the data do not leave the country (Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows, 2024). Where sensitive personal information of fewer than 10,000 individuals is provided in a calendar year, a standard contract with the overseas recipient or personal information protection certification is required; at or above 10,000 individuals, a security assessment must be declared. Counts are cumulative from 1 January of the year, and splitting the numbers to move data that should go through a security assessment onto the contract or certification route is prohibited (Measures for Personal Information Exit Certification, Article 5, in force 1 January 2026). Before any such provision the host institution must give notice, obtain separate consent where the law requires it, and carry out a personal information protection impact assessment.

Storage, minimum necessary, and records

Data stay on servers in the Chinese mainland. Access is granted to named individuals and is limited to the variables the approved manuscript needs. The host institution keeps a record of every extract, of the people named on it and of the period of access. A multi-centre study may not be split into separate applications (Article 32). Where a request involves international scientific cooperation, the Chinese unit must take part substantively throughout, all records and data must be open to the Chinese unit and backed up to it, and the two sides submit a joint report to the National Health Commission within six months after the approval or filing period ends (Articles 31 and 35). Renewal is applied for at least 30 working days before the period ends (Article 51).

The host institution decides which requirements apply to a request and carries them out. Applicants should allow time for them: ethics review alone may take up to 30 days, and a decision by the National Health Commission on an administrative license takes 20 working days from acceptance, extendable by 10 working days, with technical assessment time excluded (Articles 45–47).

iDianji

iDianji is this project’s use of StreetMind. StreetMind was designed and developed in the Horizon Europe project environMENTAL, in which Fudan University is a partner, under the leadership of Professor Gunter Schumann. It records places a person visits often, the routes they travel, and the day as they experience it, including geo-tracked emotions, actions, and behaviour. The application is described at streetmind.eu. A Chinese version, whose development was supported by the National Key R&D Program of the Ministry of Science and Technology of China (MOST 2023YFE0199700), is already in use. This project advances StreetMind by linking those reports to wearable physiology and to environmental data in environMAP. Each enrolled participant carries iDianji for four weeks, alongside a Huawei Watch GT6.

It mainly delivers three modules, on the schedule in the research plan’s data-collection section.

My Spots · weekly

Once a week, asks about a place the participant adds: what the place is, whether they are alone or with others, when they started going there, how long they spend there, how it feels, how they travel there, and a short account or photo.

My Route · weekly

Once a week, asks about a usual journey: where it starts and ends, the transport, why they chose it, conditions along the way, how long it takes, and how connected they feel to other people and to nature on that route.

My Day · daily

Each day, asks about the day as a whole: mood, sleep, activity, nutrition, who they spent time with, time in green space and by water, noise, temperature and air, and substance use.

The same application records smartphone sensing: location, activity state, accelerometer readings, and foreground application use. The Perceived Stress Scale is collected here as well: one or two items each day, and the full scale twice a week, together with AUDIT, PHQ-9, and GAD-7. An audio recording is requested once a week. Free text is released for research only after identifying details have been removed, unless a proposal gives a reason to receive the unedited text and the access-review group agrees.
