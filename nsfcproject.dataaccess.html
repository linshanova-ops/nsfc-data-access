Urban environmental stress and mental illness

Identification and molecular characterisation of urban-environmental stress patterns affecting mental illness

识别城市环境压力模式对精神障碍的影响及其分子机制

This project, grant W2541022, studies how patterns of urban and environmental stress relate to mental illness, and the molecular measures that travel with those patterns. It recruits adults in Shanghai and Beijing and follows each person through a clinical visit, four weeks of smartphone and wearable monitoring, and an end-of-study assessment.

Recruitment is under way. At the 28 September 2026 snapshot used in the data-access materials, 244 participants had been registered: 96 healthy controls, 68 with generalised anxiety disorder, 56 with major depressive disorder and 24 with alcohol use disorder.

The dataset

The NSFC dataset

The NSFC dataset is the data generated under grant W2541022. It joins four collection systems: the electronic data capture system (EDC), which holds registration, demographic and medical history, clinical follow-up, medication, adverse event, biosample and hair-sample records, and the login-code mapping; the iDianji application, which holds ecological momentary assessment and smartphone sensing; Huawei wearable devices carried through Huawei Health, which hold continuous physiology, sleep, physical activity and location; and the biosample and omics pipelines. Each system writes its own tables with its own participant key. The coordinating centre holds the mapping between keys and issues research extracts in which direct identifiers are replaced by an anonymous participant code.

Recruitment is under way and the dataset grows every week. At the time this version of the policy was prepared, 244 participants had been registered at the three centres between 25 February and 28 September 2026: 96 healthy controls, 68 with generalised anxiety disorder, 56 with major depressive disorder and 24 with alcohol use disorder. Of these, 142 had completed the study, 68 were in follow-up, 21 had left the study and 13 were still in screening. Registration by centre was Huashan Hospital of Fudan University 88, Peking University Sixth Hospital 84, and Shanghai Mental Health Center 72.

Coverage is uneven across streams. Not every participant contributes to every stream, and 1-minute wearable streams start later than the 10-minute streams. Applicants should request the counts for the variables they intend to use before they fix an analysis plan, because the number with complete data is smaller than the number registered.

Demographics

Demographics

Basic demographic variables are scattered across the registration record, the demographic and medical history form, the application account record and the follow-up assessments. They are not held in one table, and applicants should expect to assemble them from more than one source.

Age

Recorded as age in years at assessment in the demographic history form, and as year of birth in the registration record. Because the study has repeated assessments, age belongs to an assessment rather than to the participant: the same participant has an age at baseline and an age at each follow-up.

Sex

Recorded in the registration record (Male/Female) and again in the application account record (M/F).

Education, occupation and marital status

Recorded in the demographic history form, together with handedness, height, weight, place of origin and the district of residence.

Recruitment centre

Recorded in the registration record as the centre that enrolled the participant, and repeated on the biosample records.

Group

Recorded at registration as healthy control, generalised anxiety disorder, major depressive disorder or alcohol use disorder. The clinical diagnosis recorded at assessment is also held in the demographic history form.

Study design

Study design

The design below is the approved research plan. It is the target for the study as a whole and not a description of what has been collected so far. The counts in the sections above are the ones that apply to a request.

The project will recruit 680 adults aged 18–50 years, with a balanced sex distribution, from outpatient services in Shanghai and Beijing. There are four groups of 170 participants: major depressive disorder, anxiety disorder, alcohol use disorder, and healthy controls. Diagnoses follow ICD-10 or DSM-5. Comorbidity is allowed. Recruitment centres are Huashan Hospital of Fudan University, Shanghai Mental Health Center, and Peking University Sixth Hospital. The research plan assigns recruitment of 253 participants to each of Shanghai Mental Health Center and Peking University Sixth Hospital.

Each enrolled participant completes a baseline clinical visit, four weeks of smartphone and wearable monitoring, and an end-of-study assessment. Blood (24 mL, in EDTA, heparin, and Tempus tubes) and urine are collected once, at the start of the study. The released research dataset uses a unique anonymous code on every record. Names, contact details, and government identity numbers are not part of the dataset provided for analysis.

This policy covers data generated by grant W2541022. It does not grant access to ZIB, IMAGEN, or STRATIFY. Each of those resources has its own application route and its own approvals. Approval of this form does not approve those datasets. The NSFC research plan names them only as external validation cohorts.

Demographics

Age, sex, education, occupation, marital status, height, weight, and recruitment centre.

Clinical characterisation

MINI diagnostic interview; medical, surgical, family, and social history; allergies; current and past medication, including dose and duration.

Repeated psychological measures

AUDIT, PHQ-9, and GAD-7 twice weekly; cue-induced craving on a visual analogue scale; the Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress at the beginning and end; one or two Perceived Stress Scale items daily and the full scale twice weekly; surveys of urbanicity, eco-anxiety, and mental resilience; participant feedback.

iDianji ecological momentary assessment

My Spots, My Route, and My Day; Likert ratings of emotional state; attribution of heart-rate changes; activity and social context; free text; self-initiated prompts; heart-rate-triggered prompts; and random control prompts. Wearable physiology

Huawei Watch GT6. Heart rate by photoplethysmography at 1-minute and 10-minute resolution; blood oxygen saturation; a device stress index reported hourly; nightly sleep summary with stages; steps, distance and energy expenditure; physical activity sessions with duration, heart-rate range, cadence and VO2max; and device location with altitude.

Location and environmental exposure

Latitude and longitude time series from GPS, Wi-Fi, and mobile-network location, linked by authorised staff to environMAP layers for urbanicity and land cover, natural space, climate and weather, air pollution, and regional socioeconomic status.

Multi-omics

Illumina Chinese Genotyping Array genotypes; Illumina EPIC DNA methylation (about 935,000 sites); Illumina NovaSeq RNA sequencing; and plasma proteomics by mass spectrometry or antibody-based assays.

Governance

Legal and regulatory requirements

Release of these data is governed by the law of the People’s Republic of China. The host institution holds the data and carries the statutory duties. Approval by the access-review group is a scientific approval. It does not by itself satisfy a statutory requirement, and no extract is released until the host institution has cleared the requirements that apply to that request.

The host institution decides which requirements apply to a request and carries them out. Applicants should allow time for them: ethics review alone may take up to 30 days, and a Ministry decision on human genetic resource information takes longer.

Ethics review of the secondary use

Reusing participant data is research involving human participants. The institution that provides the data must have the proposed use reviewed by its ethics committee and must fix by agreement the scope of use, how the data are handled, and how they are disposed of at the end (Measures for the Ethics Review of Life Science and Medical Research Involving Humans, 2023, Article 28). Where a study qualifies for expedited review or for exemption, the committee records that decision; exemption does not apply where sensitive personal information is involved. The manuscript must cite the approval that covers this use (Article 29). State an ethics approval number on the form; that number is confirmed by the host institution at the time of access.

Human genetic resource information

Under the Implementing Rules of the Regulation on the Administration of Human Genetic Resources (Ministry of Science and Technology Order No. 21, in force 1 July 2023, Article 2), human genetic resource information is human gene and genome data. It does not include clinical data, imaging data, protein data or metabolic data. In this dataset, genotypes, DNA methylation and RNA sequencing are treated as human genetic resource information. Plasma proteomics is not, but it remains sensitive personal information. Planned enrolment of 680 participants is below the 3,000-participant threshold at which a collection licence would be required (Article 27).

Provision to an overseas recipient

Only a Chinese unit may provide Chinese human genetic resources to an overseas recipient. An overseas organisation, or an institution established or actually controlled by one, may not collect, preserve or provide them (Article 11). Providing or opening human genetic resource information to such a recipient requires prior reporting to the Ministry of Science and Technology and submission of an information backup (Article 36). It must also pass the security review organised by the Ministry where the information may affect public health, national security or the public interest, including exome or genome sequencing information on more than 500 persons (Article 37). With 680 participants, the genomic data in this project are above the 500-person threshold, so a request that gives an overseas recipient genomic data is treated as requiring security review until the Ministry decides otherwise.

Personal information and cross-border access

A psychiatric diagnosis and a location trajectory are sensitive personal information under the Personal Information Protection Law. Letting an overseas body access or process data held on a server in China counts as providing data overseas, even though the data do not leave the country (Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows, 2024). Where sensitive personal information of fewer than 10,000 individuals is provided in a calendar year, a standard contract with the overseas recipient or personal information protection certification is required; at or above 10,000 individuals, a security assessment must be declared. Before any such provision the host institution must give notice, obtain separate consent where the law requires it, and carry out a personal information protection impact assessment.

Storage, minimum necessary, and records

Data stay on servers in the Chinese mainland. Access is granted to named individuals and is limited to the variables the approved manuscript needs. The host institution keeps a record of every extract, of the people named on it and of the period of access. Where a request involves international scientific cooperation, the Chinese unit must take part substantively throughout, all records and data must be open to the Chinese unit and backed up to it, and the two sides submit a joint report on the cooperation within six months after the approval or filing period ends (Articles 14 and 35).

Accounts, and access from outside the Chinese mainland

After approval:

  1. The Principal Investigator, or a data contact the Principal Investigator designates, opens an account only for each person named on the proposal. Accounts are personal and are closed at the end of the approved period.
  2. Access is limited to the approved groups, centres, time points, and variables.
  3. If any named user is outside the Chinese mainland, or if the proposal asks to export participant-level data, access is not opened until the host institution has completed the data-transfer arrangement required for that transfer. V

How approved data are provided

The research plan states that electronic data are stored on secure password-protected servers, with access limited to authorised members of the research team. After approval, the Principal Investigator, or a data contact the Principal Investigator designates, sends each named user a username, a password, and the address of that server. The address is not published in this policy.

Users must not place participant-level data on personal cloud storage, shared drives, or removable media, and must not pass data or passwords to anyone who is not named on the proposal. Environmental linkage to environMAP is carried out inside the project’s spatial-data infrastructure, or by authorised staff, as stated in the research plan. Applicants ordinarily receive the linked exposure variables, not a separate copy of the environMAP catalogue.

Application

iDianji

iDianji is the project’s ecological momentary assessment and smartphone-sensing application. Each enrolled participant carries it for four weeks, alongside a Huawei Watch GT6.

The application delivers My Spots, My Route, and My Day. Participants rate emotional state, attribute heart-rate changes to emotion, activity, or an unknown cause, and report recent activity and social context. They can add free text and a sleep report. Prompts are self-initiated, triggered when heart rate departs from the participant’s baseline, or delivered as random control prompts.

The same application records smartphone sensing: location, activity state, accelerometer readings, and foreground application use. It also delivers questionnaire instances, including the Perceived Stress Scale, and can request an audio recording. Free text is released for research only after identifying details have been removed, unless a proposal gives a reason to receive the unedited text and the access-review group agrees.

In the working dataset through 28 September 2026, iDianji had delivered 67,143 questionnaire instances to 207 participants and received 8,863 submissions from 196 participants, with 295,838 item-level responses. Geocoded locations covered 149 participants. Device terminal records covered 205 participants.

Associate Professor Hang Su leads ecological momentary assessment. Professor Tianye Jia works on ecological momentary assessment and geospatial analysis. Professor Frauke Nees is named on the proposal form for iDianji and smartphone sensing.
